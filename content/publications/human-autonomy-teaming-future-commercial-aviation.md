This book chapter examines human–autonomy teaming for future single-pilot commercial aviation. It develops a case study around a bird strike and engine failure during initial climb, where an onboard autonomous system works with the pilot to manage the emergency.

The chapter follows a structured process: define operational constraints, analyze the task, identify human and autonomy roles, explore function-allocation alternatives, derive teaming requirements, and plan system evaluation. Interdependence Analysis is used to compare ways of organizing the joint activity and to identify observability, predictability, and directability requirements.

The case study connects early human-centred analysis to prototype development, cognitive modelling, and human-in-the-loop simulation.
